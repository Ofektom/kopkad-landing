"I want to save more" is a wish. "I'll save ₦20,000 a month into a separate account and have ₦240,000 for my emergency fund by next December" is a plan.

The second one is far more likely to happen. The difference has a name: a SMART goal.

## What SMART means

- **Specific:** say exactly what it's for. Not "save money", but "my emergency fund".
- **Measurable:** put a number on it, so you can track progress. ₦240,000.
- **Achievable:** check it against your real income and spending from Topic 2. A goal that needs 80% of your income will be abandoned.
- **Relevant:** it should serve your values from earlier this week, and the stage of life you're in.
- **Time-bound:** give it a date. "By 31 December next year."

!> A SMART goal answers: what, how much, by when, and why.

## Before and after

**Wish:** "I want to clear my debts."
**SMART:** "I'll pay off my ₦85,000 loan app balance first, with ₦30,000 a month, so it's cleared by the end of March. Then I'll move that ₦30,000 to the next debt."

**Wish:** "I want to start a business someday."
**SMART:** "I'll save ₦78,400 a month to have about ₦2.2 million of shop capital by December 2028, so I can work for myself."

**Wish:** "I want to own a home."
**SMART:** "I'll save ₦50,000 a month towards a ₦3 million land deposit by 2031, and check the price every year."

## Checking "achievable"

This is the letter people skip. To check it:

1. Take your surplus from Topic 2: what's left each month.
2. Add up the monthly amounts all your goals need.
3. If the goals need more than your surplus, something has to change: a later date, a smaller first target, more income, or less spending.

It's better to set a goal you reach and then raise it than to set one you abandon in March.

## Write it where you'll see it

A goal written down, with its monthly amount, is much more likely to happen than one kept in your head. Put it on your phone's lock screen, the fridge or the first page of your notebook.
