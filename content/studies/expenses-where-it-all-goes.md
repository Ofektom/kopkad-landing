Most people know their big bills: rent, school fees, the car loan. What surprises them, when they finally track a month of spending, is everything else.

A ₦1,500 snack here, ₦200 transfer fees there, data top-ups, a ride because it was raining, a small contribution for a colleague's birthday. None of it feels like much. Added up, it's often the difference between saving and not saving.

!> Small leaks sink big ships.

## Two ways to sort your spending

**Fixed or variable?**

- **Fixed expenses** come whether you like it or not, usually for the same amount: rent, school fees, loan repayments, subscriptions.
- **Variable expenses** change with your choices: food, transport, outings, gifts, clothing.

We look at each type in the next two studies.

**Needs or wants?**

- **Needs** keep you safe, housed, fed, healthy and able to earn.
- **Wants** make life nicer but aren't essential.

The line isn't always sharp. Food is a need; eating out every day is mostly a want. Transport to work is a need; a ride instead of the bus is partly a want. The point isn't to cut every want, but to know which is which before you pay.

## The hidden costs

These are the expenses people forget to count:

- **Data and airtime:** often several small top-ups instead of one plan.
- **Bank and transfer charges:** small per transaction, large over a month.
- **Transport extras:** the rides you didn't plan for.
- **Small treats:** drinks, snacks, sweets for the children.
- **Giving and contributions:** family requests, office collections, events.
- **Subscriptions:** streaming, TV, apps you no longer use.

## An example

Daniel was sure he spent about ₦10,000 a month on "small things". He tracked one month. The real figure:

- Data top-ups: ₦14,000
- Snacks and drinks: ₦18,500
- Transfer and bank charges: ₦3,200
- Unplanned rides: ₦12,000

That's **₦47,700**, almost five times his guess. He didn't cut it all out. He switched to a monthly data plan and planned his rides, and kept ₦15,000 a month that used to disappear.
