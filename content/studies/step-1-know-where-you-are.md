Quick test: how much did you spend last month? Not roughly. The actual figure.

Most people can't answer. That isn't a moral failing; nobody taught us to look. But it means every money decision we make is a guess. Step 1 of the journey fixes that.

## Your five numbers

Knowing where you are means knowing five things:

1. **Income:** everything that comes in during a month. Salary, business takings, side work, money from family.
2. **Expenses:** everything that goes out. Rent, food, transport, data, school fees, family support, the small daily things.
3. **Savings:** income minus expenses. If it's negative, you're filling the gap with borrowing or savings you already had.
4. **What you own (assets):** money saved, money owed to you, and things with real resale value, like land, a car, equipment for your business.
5. **What you owe (debts):** loans, credit, money borrowed from friends or family, unpaid bills.

From the last two comes the most honest number in personal finance:

!> Net worth = what you own − what you owe.

## An example

Michael earns ₦300,000 a month and spends about ₦280,000, so he saves around ₦20,000.

He owns ₦120,000 in savings and a laptop he could sell for about ₦180,000: ₦300,000 in total. He owes ₦200,000 on a loan he took for his rent.

His net worth is ₦300,000 − ₦200,000 = **₦100,000**.

That number isn't good or bad. It's his starting point. Next year, if his net worth is higher, he's moving in the right direction, whatever his salary does.

## Income and cash are not the same

One trap to watch: money you've earned isn't always money in your hand. A customer who owes you, a salary that arrives late, a contribution that pays out next month. When you list your numbers, note what's actually in your account versus what's still coming. Plans fail on cash, not on promises.

## Clarity, not guilt

When people first add up their spending, the usual reaction is shock, then guilt. Skip the guilt. You can't change last month. What you can do is use it: now you know where the money really goes, you can decide where it should go.

## Do it this week

Use the free **Money Map** worksheet below, or a notebook. Go through your bank app or mobile money history for the last 30 days and write down every inflow and outflow. It takes about 20 minutes, and you'll know more about your money than most people ever do.

**Rate yourself:** before you start, how clear are you about your money, from 1 to 10? Check again when you're done.
