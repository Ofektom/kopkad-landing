Imagine planning a trip from Lagos to Abuja without knowing where you're starting from. You might have a great car and plenty of fuel, but you'd still get lost.

Money works the same way. Most people try to jump straight to the exciting part (investing, a business idea, a big purchase) without knowing where they stand or exactly where they're going. Financial intelligence treats money as a journey, with six steps.

## The six steps

1. **Know where you are.** What comes in, what goes out, what you own and what you owe. Your starting point.
2. **Choose your destination.** The goals you want to reach: for your home, your family, your work and your future. Each with a price and a date.
3. **Find the gap.** The difference between where you'll be if nothing changes and where you want to be.
4. **Plan the route.** How you'll close that gap: earn more, spend less, save regularly, plan for tax, invest wisely.
5. **Watch the risks.** What could knock you off the road: losing a job, illness, rising prices, a bad investment.
6. **Walk it, together.** Act on the plan with discipline, and keep the people you live with on the same page.

!> You can't plan a route until you know your starting point.

## Why the order matters

Skipping steps is how good intentions fail.

- People who skip **step 1** set goals that don't fit their real income, then give up.
- People who skip **step 2** save with no target, so any "emergency" can empty the savings.
- People who skip **step 5** build a plan that collapses the first time life goes wrong.
- People who skip **step 6** make a beautiful plan alone, then watch a partner or relative undo it.

## How this class follows the journey

Over the next week we'll take one step a day. After that, every topic in the class fits somewhere on this road: budgeting and tracking help you with step 1, emergency funds and protection help with step 5, investing and income help with step 4, and so on. If you ever wonder why a topic matters, come back to this map.
