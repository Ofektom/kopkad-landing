Have you ever had a raise and, three months later, felt no richer? You're not alone. It has a name: lifestyle creep.

## How it happens

The raise lands. A slightly bigger apartment. A better phone on instalments. Eating out a bit more, because you can. Each change is small and reasonable. Together they swallow the whole raise, and soon the new spending feels normal. Your savings stay exactly where they were.

## Michael's raise

Michael's salary goes from ₦300,000 to ₦360,000 a month. After pension, NHF and PAYE under the current tax rules, his take-home rises from about ₦242,500 to about **₦287,200**. That's around ₦44,600 more each month.

Two ways it could go:

- **Creep:** a nicer flat with ₦30,000 more rent a month, plus a phone on instalments. Within three months, all of the ₦44,600 is spoken for.
- **Plan:** half of the increase, ₦22,300, goes straight to his Future slice. The rest is his to enjoy. His Living slice still grows, but his savings grow faster.

Over a year, the planned version adds about **₦268,000** to his future. Over ten years of raises, the gap between the two Michaels becomes enormous.

## The rule

!> When income rises, raise the Future slice first, then the Living slice.

A simple version: **save at least half of every raise.** Move the new amount into your Future slice from the first payday, before you get used to having it.

## Enjoy some of it

This isn't about refusing every upgrade. Celebrate a raise, and let your life get a little better. The point is to decide how much, rather than letting it creep.

## Your turn

Think about your last raise or income increase. Where did it go: savings or lifestyle? Decide now what share of your next increase will go to your Future slice.
