By now you probably have more goals than money. Rent, an emergency fund, a debt, school fees, a business, a home. Trying to fund all of them a little at a time usually means none of them gets there.

You need an order.

## A sensible order

**1. A small safety buffer.** Before anything else, a starter emergency fund: even ₦50,000 to ₦100,000, or about one month of essential spending. This stops the next small emergency from turning into a new loan.

**2. Expensive debt.** Loan apps and other high-cost borrowing can cost far more in charges than any savings will earn. Paying them off is often the best "return" available to you. (Cheaper, planned debt, like a low-interest loan for equipment that earns, can be paid on schedule.)

**3. Your full emergency fund.** Build up to three, then six, months of essential spending. We cover this properly in Topic 9.

**4. Your goals, by date and importance.** Goals that are due soon and must happen (rent, school fees) come before goals that are later or more flexible (a car, a holiday).

**5. Long-term growth.** Long-term saving and investing, for a home, your children's future and financial independence. Start this early, even small, once the steps above are under way.

!> Safety first. Then stop the debt that's costing you. Then build towards your goals.

## Not strictly one at a time

You don't have to finish one step completely before touching the next. Many people put most of their surplus into the current step and a small amount into a later one, especially long-term saving, so the habit and the snowball start early.

## An example

Daniel has a surplus of ₦40,000 a month. His priorities:

- **Now:** ₦10,000 to a starter emergency fund until it reaches ₦60,000, and ₦30,000 to his ₦85,000 loan app balance.
- **After about 3 months:** the loan app is cleared and the starter fund is halfway there. Of the ₦30,000 that went to the loan app, ₦25,000 moves to his next debt and ₦5,000 starts going to long-term savings, just to get the habit going.
- **Next:** finishing the starter fund, then building his full emergency fund and saving for his rent due in a year.

Without an order, he'd have spread ₦40,000 across six things and felt like he was getting nowhere.

## When family needs come up

Requests from family are part of life for most of us. Decide in advance how much you can give each month and treat it as its own line, not as something taken from your emergency fund. That way you can be generous without falling back to the start.
