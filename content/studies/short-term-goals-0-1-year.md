Some goals can't wait. The rent is due in eight months. The school fees are due in January. These are short-term goals: anything you need within the next year.

## Typical short-term goals

- the next rent payment,
- school fees for the coming term or year,
- a starter emergency fund,
- clearing a small, expensive debt, like a loan app,
- a planned event: a wedding contribution, a naming ceremony, travel home for the holidays,
- replacing a phone or laptop you rely on for work.

## Pricing and dating them

Short-term goals are the easiest to price, because you usually know the cost already.

1. **Write the amount.** If it's a quote, check it's current.
2. **Write the date** you need the money by, not when you'd like to start.
3. **Subtract what you've already saved** towards it.
4. **Divide what's left by the months remaining.**

## An example

Esther's rent of **₦600,000** is due in **8 months**. She's already put aside **₦120,000**.

₦600,000 − ₦120,000 = ₦480,000 still needed.
₦480,000 ÷ 8 months = **₦60,000 a month**.

If ₦60,000 a month isn't possible, she knows now, eight months ahead, while there's still time to talk to her landlord, find extra income or adjust.

!> Short-term money must be safe and easy to reach. This is not money to gamble with.

## Where to keep it

Money you'll need within a year has one job: be there when you need it, in full.

- **Keep it separate** from your spending account, so it doesn't leak away.
- **Keep it safe.** A savings account or savings plan with a licensed bank or provider.
- **Keep it reachable** without big penalties, especially your emergency fund.
- **Don't chase high returns** with it. Shares, crypto and "investment schemes" can fall just when you need the money, and anything promising guaranteed high returns is a red flag.

Interest on short-term savings is a bonus, not the point.
