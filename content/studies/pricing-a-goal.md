Most goals end up costing more than we planned. Not because we're bad at maths, but because we price them at today's sticker price and forget everything else.

Pricing a goal properly takes five steps.

## The five steps

1. **Today's price.** Get a real quote or check current prices.
2. **Allow for rising prices.** For a rough figure, add about 15% for each year until you'll buy it. Nigeria's official inflation rate was about 14.6% in September 2026; check the latest figure and adjust.
3. **Add the extras.** Delivery, installation, registration, legal fees, agent fees, the first month's running costs. These are easy to forget and rarely small.
4. **Add a buffer.** About 10% for surprises.
5. **Subtract what you have, and divide by the months.** That's your monthly amount.

!> Price the goal you'll actually pay for, in the year you'll actually pay for it.

## A worked example

Esther wants two industrial sewing machines to grow her tailoring business. They cost **₦900,000** today, and she plans to buy them in **2 years**.

1. Today's price: **₦900,000**.
2. Rising prices over 2 years: ₦900,000 × 1.15 × 1.15 = **₦1,190,250**.
3. Delivery and installation: **₦60,000**, making ₦1,250,250.
4. A 10% buffer: **₦1,375,275**.
5. She's already saved **₦200,000**, so she needs ₦1,175,275 more over 24 months: about **₦49,000 a month**.

If she'd only saved towards ₦900,000, she'd have been nearly ₦500,000 short when the day came.

## What if the monthly amount is too high?

Once you've priced it honestly, the monthly figure might be more than you can manage. You have options:

- **Move the date.** Three years instead of two lowers the monthly amount, though rising prices push the total up a little.
- **Start smaller.** One machine now, a second later.
- **Close the gap another way.** Extra income, or cutting a cost, as in Topic 1's five routes.
- **Grow the money.** For goals more than a year away, savings that earn interest reduce what you need to put in.

## Check it every year

Prices don't rise neatly. Once a year, check the real price again and adjust your monthly amount. A goal that's a little behind is easy to fix; one that's never checked is a surprise waiting to happen.
