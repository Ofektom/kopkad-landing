Long-term goals are the big ones, five years away or more:

- owning your home,
- your children's university education,
- building a business that runs without you,
- the day you can stop working, or choose to, without worrying about money.

They can feel so far away and so expensive that it's tempting not to think about them at all. That's exactly why most people never reach them.

## Time is the advantage

Long-term goals have one big advantage: time. And time does two things for you.

**1. Small amounts add up.** ₦10,000 a month for 20 years is ₦2,400,000 before any growth at all.

**2. Money can grow on its growth.** If savings are put where they earn a return, the return starts earning too. That's the snowball.

As an illustration only (real returns go up and down, and nobody can promise them): if that ₦10,000 a month grew at 10% a year for 20 years, it would come to about **₦7.6 million**. More than three times what was put in, and most of the difference comes in the later years.

!> For long-term goals, the most powerful thing you can do is start early, even small.

## Starting early versus starting big

Two people each save ₦10,000 a month until 60, and as an illustration their money grows at 10% a year:

- **Michael** starts at 25. By 60 he has about **₦38 million**.
- **Daniel** starts at 35. By 60 he has about **₦13 million**.

Michael only saved ₦1.2 million more than Daniel, over his extra ten years. But he ends up with nearly three times as much, because his money had ten more years to grow.

## Prices rise over long horizons too

Over ten years or more, rising prices matter a lot. University fees of ₦1,000,000 a year today could cost around ₦4 million a year in ten years if prices keep rising at about 15% a year. That's why long-term money usually needs to be in things that can grow faster than prices over time, such as some investments, rather than sitting in a current account. We'll cover how investing works, and how to avoid the traps, in Topic 18.

## Don't freeze

Long-term numbers can look impossible. Don't let that stop you. Start with an amount you can keep up, raise it every time your income rises, and check your progress each year. Something small started now beats something perfect started "when things settle down".
