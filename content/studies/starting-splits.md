How big should each slice be? There's no perfect answer, but there are good places to start. The Wealth Builder in the Kopkad app uses three tiers.

## The three tiers

- **Seed**: Living 70%, Future 20%, Generosity 10%
- **Grow**: Living 60%, Future 25%, Generosity 15%
- **Harvest**: Living 50%, Future 30%, Generosity 20%

- **Seed** is where most people should begin.
- **Grow** fits once you track your spending and have started cutting costs.
- **Harvest** suits people with low fixed costs and a strong saving habit.

## What that means in naira

On a take-home pay of ₦200,000:

- Seed: Living ₦140,000, Future ₦40,000, Generosity ₦20,000
- Grow: Living ₦120,000, Future ₦50,000, Generosity ₦30,000
- Harvest: Living ₦100,000, Future ₦60,000, Generosity ₦40,000

## The carry-over

Whatever is left in your Generosity slice at the end of the period moves into your emergency fund. On Harvest, if you give half your Generosity slice and carry the rest over, half of your income goes to your future.

## If even Seed is too much

Start lower. 10% to the future and 5% to giving is a real start. Then raise your Future slice by a few points every two or three months, as you cut costs or earn more. The habit matters more than the percentage.

!> Start where you are. Move up a tier when your costs fall or your income rises, not before.

## When to move up

- your Living costs have fitted comfortably for three months in a row,
- your starter emergency fund is in place,
- or your income has gone up.
