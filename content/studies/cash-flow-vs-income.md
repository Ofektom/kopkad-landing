A trader sells ₦500,000 of goods this month and makes a good profit on paper. But half her customers haven't paid yet, her supplier wants cash today, and she can't pay her rent.

She isn't unprofitable. She has a cash flow problem. And plenty of households have the same problem without realising it.

## Income and cash flow are different

- **Income and expenses** tell you whether you're earning more than you spend over time.
- **Cash flow** tells you what actually moves in and out of your account, and when.

Most of the time they line up. But when they don't, it's cash flow that decides whether you can pay your bills this week.

!> Plans are made on income. Bills are paid with cash.

## Where they come apart

**Loans.** When you borrow ₦200,000, cash comes in, but it isn't income. You haven't earned it; you owe it. Treating a loan as income is one of the most common money mistakes: it feels like a good month, so spending rises, and the repayments arrive later.

**Loan repayments.** When you repay a loan, cash goes out, but most of it isn't an expense. It's reducing a debt. Only the interest and fees are a true cost.

**Money owed to you.** A customer who owes you, a salary that's late, a contribution that pays out next month: it's income you've earned, but you can't spend it yet.

**Big yearly bills.** Rent or school fees paid once a year are an expense spread over the whole year, but the cash leaves your account all at once.

## An example

Last month Michael's account showed:

- salary: ₦242,500,
- a ₦100,000 loan from a loan app,
- spending of ₦230,000,
- a ₦30,000 loan repayment, of which ₦8,000 was interest.

His cash went up by ₦82,500, so it felt like a great month. But his real surplus was only his salary minus his spending and the interest: ₦242,500 − ₦230,000 − ₦8,000 = **₦4,500**. The rest of the extra cash was borrowed, and it has to go back.

## Managing cash flow

- **Keep a buffer** in your account so a late payment doesn't become a crisis.
- **Spread big yearly bills:** save a twelfth of them every month.
- **Never count borrowed money as income** when you plan.
- **Chase what you're owed:** money owed to you isn't cash until it arrives.
