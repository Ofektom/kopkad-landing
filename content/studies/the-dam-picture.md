Here's a simple picture that puts all of Topic 2 together.

Imagine a dam. Water flows in from a river at the top. Water flows out through the gates at the bottom. The level of the water behind the dam is what you've built up.

## The dam is your money

- **The water flowing in** is your income: salary, business, investments, other income.
- **The water flowing out** is your expenses: fixed and variable.
- **The water level** is your net worth: what you own minus what you owe.

!> The level only rises if more flows in than flows out, and it has to happen month after month.

## What the picture teaches

**A bigger river isn't enough.** If the gates are wide open, a big income runs straight through and the level never rises. That's the high earner with nothing saved.

**Small leaks matter.** A dam with lots of small cracks loses as much as one big open gate. Those are the data top-ups, snacks, transfer fees and unplanned rides from earlier this topic.

**Borrowing isn't rain.** A loan pours water in today, but it's water borrowed from further downstream. When it's paid back, the level drops again. It looks like the dam is filling; it isn't.

**A drought will come.** Every income has dry seasons: a lost job, a slow business month. A dam with a high level can survive a dry season. An empty one runs dry at once. That's why the emergency fund matters.

**A higher level fills itself.** Once there's enough water behind the dam, some of it can work for you: savings earning interest, investments paying returns. That's a second river flowing in, which raises the level faster. It's the snowball from Topic 5.

## Three ways to raise the level

1. **Widen the river:** earn more, add income sources.
2. **Narrow the gates:** control fixed costs, cut variable spending, close the leaks.
3. **Add a second river:** put savings to work so your money earns money.

Is your dam rising or leaking right now? Look at your surplus from earlier this topic and your net worth. Which of the three ways could you work on first?
