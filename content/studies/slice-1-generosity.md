For most of us, giving isn't optional. Parents, siblings, church or mosque, the friend getting married, the neighbour in hospital. The question isn't whether you give, but whether you plan it.

## What goes in the Generosity slice

- **Family support:** money to parents, school fees for a sibling, help for relatives.
- **Religious giving:** tithes, offerings, zakat, sadaqah.
- **Community:** contributions, weddings, naming ceremonies, funerals, a gift for a colleague.

## Why give from a plan

When giving is the leftover, two things happen. In good months, you give a lot and save nothing. In tight months, you give nothing and feel guilty, or you give from money meant for rent.

When giving has its own slice:

- you give every month, not only when you feel rich,
- you can say yes without wondering what you just broke,
- and your savings stay protected.

!> A planned gift and an unplanned gift help the same person. Only one of them also protects your future.

## Grace's Generosity slice

Grace sets aside 10% of her ₦200,000, which is **₦20,000** a month:

- ₦10,000 to her mother on the first Saturday of the month,
- ₦6,000 for religious giving,
- ₦4,000 kept for community events: a wedding, a naming, a contribution.

If no wedding comes up this month, the ₦4,000 isn't spent just because it's there. In the Wealth Builder, any Give money left at the end of the period moves into your emergency fund, so unused generosity still works for you.

## If your faith asks for a set amount

If you give a tithe of 10%, that is your Generosity slice at the very least. Plan the rest of your pie around it: it's better to have a smaller Living slice on purpose than to keep missing your giving or your saving.
