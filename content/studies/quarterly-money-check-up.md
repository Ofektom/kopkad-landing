Knowing where you stand isn't a one-time job. Your income changes, prices rise, debts shrink, savings grow. A map from last year can send you the wrong way.

The answer isn't to obsess over your money every day. It's a short, regular check-up, the way you'd see a doctor for a routine visit.

## When

Every three months. The easy dates to remember are the end of **March, June, September and December**. Pick a quiet weekend, put it in your phone calendar now, and treat it like any other appointment.

!> Twenty minutes, four times a year. That's all it takes to always know where you stand.

## The 20-minute routine

1. **Update your income** (5 minutes). What came in each month this quarter? Any new sources, or any that stopped?
2. **Check your spending** (5 minutes). Look at your bank and mobile money history. What were your fixed costs, and where did the variable spending go? Any new leaks?
3. **Update what you own** (3 minutes). Savings, pension, investments, and anything else at today's value.
4. **Update what you owe** (3 minutes). The balance left on every debt.
5. **Work out your net worth** (2 minutes). Write it next to last quarter's.
6. **Ask two questions** (2 minutes). Is the number going up? What's one thing I'll do differently next quarter?

## Keep a record

The check-up is most useful when you can compare. Keep every quarter's figures in one place: a notebook, the Money Map, or the free **Net Worth Calculator** spreadsheet below, which keeps a quarterly history so you can see the trend at a glance.

## Do it together

If you share money with a partner or family, make the check-up a joint meeting. It's a calm, planned time to talk about money, away from arguments and emergencies. Step 6 of the journey, walking it together, gets much easier when you have a regular time for it.

## What to look for

- **Net worth rising:** keep going. Consider raising your savings rate a little.
- **Net worth flat:** look for the leak. Usually it's variable spending or a new debt.
- **Net worth falling:** don't panic. Find the cause, then fix one thing before next quarter.

## Your turn

Pick the date of your next check-up and put it in your calendar now. The end of December is a good place to start.
