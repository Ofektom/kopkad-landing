If you're paid a fixed salary, planning is fairly simple: the same amount arrives on the same day. But millions of us don't earn like that. Traders, artisans, drivers, freelancers, business owners: income rises and falls, sometimes week to week.

That doesn't make planning impossible. It means planning differently.

## The trap

The danger with irregular income is planning on your best month. A trader has a great December and decides she can afford a bigger shop. Then January is slow, February is slower, and the rent is still due.

Good months feel like the "real" income. They're not. The low months are what you have to survive on.

!> Plan on your low month. Save the good months.

## Three steps

**1. Find your average.** Write down what you earned (take-home, after business costs) for each of the last 3 to 6 months. Add them up and divide by the number of months.

**2. Find your floor.** Look at the lowest month in that list. That's the income you plan your essential spending on: rent, food, school fees, transport.

**3. Save the difference.** In any month you earn more than your floor, the extra goes into savings first. That savings pot then carries you through the slow months.

## An example

Grace sells foodstuffs at the market. Her last six months, after costs:

- ₦240,000, ₦180,000, ₦310,000, ₦200,000, ₦260,000, ₦290,000

Her average is **₦246,667**. Her lowest month was **₦180,000**.

So Grace plans her essential monthly spending around ₦180,000. In a ₦290,000 month, ₦110,000 is extra. She saves most of it, so when a ₦180,000 month comes, she doesn't have to borrow.

## Pay yourself a salary

Some business owners go one step further: they keep business money in one account and pay themselves a fixed amount every month, like a salary. Good months build up the business account; slow months are covered from it. Your personal life gets steady, even when your trade isn't.

## Track as you go

With irregular income, you can't know your average unless you write it down. Record what comes in every day or every week, even roughly. After a few months, the pattern appears, and so does your floor.

## Your turn

Is your income fixed, or does it change? If it changes, write down your last three months and find your floor.
