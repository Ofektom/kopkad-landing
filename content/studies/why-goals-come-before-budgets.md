Topic 2 showed you exactly where you stand. Topic 3 is about where you want to go: step 2 of the journey, choosing your destination.

You might expect a money class to jump straight to budgeting. We're doing goals first, on purpose.

## Why budgets fail

Most people who try a budget give up within a few weeks. Not because they can't do the maths, but because a budget on its own feels like punishment. Every line says "no": no outing, no new clothes, no treat. After a while, the natural response is to stop listening.

Now compare two people cutting their eating out from ₦40,000 to ₦15,000 a month.

- **Daniel** does it because "you're supposed to budget." By week three he's tired of saying no.
- **Esther** does it because the ₦25,000 she saves each month is going towards her shop. Every time she skips a takeaway, she's one step closer.

Same cut. Completely different experience.

!> A budget tells your money where to go. A goal tells you why.

## What goals do for you

- **They give saving a purpose.** "Save ₦25,000 a month" is hard. "Save ₦25,000 a month so I can open my shop next December" is something you can hold on to.
- **They make choices easier.** When you're tempted, the question changes from "can I afford this?" to "is this worth more to me than my goal?"
- **They protect your savings.** Money with no name attached gets spent on the first "emergency". Money labelled "rent, due January" is harder to touch.
- **They let you measure progress.** You can see how far you've come, which keeps you going.

## What makes a goal work

You met the basics in Topic 1: a goal needs a number, a date and a reason. Over the next two weeks we'll build on that:

- matching your goals to your **values**, so they're truly yours,
- sorting them into **short, medium and long term**,
- thinking about the **stages of life**,
- making them **SMART**,
- **pricing** them properly, including rising prices,
- and deciding **which comes first** when you can't fund everything at once.

By the end of the topic you'll have a written goal sheet, and that's what the budgeting topics after this will be built around.
