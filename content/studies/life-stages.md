At 22, the big money question might be how to stop running out before payday. At 35 it might be school fees and a home. At 55 it's whether you'll be able to stop working. Same person, very different goals.

Thinking in life stages helps in two ways: you focus on what matters now, and you start early on what's coming next.

## The stages

Everyone's path is different. These are typical, not rules.

**Starting out (roughly your 20s)**

- Learn to live below your income, and build the saving habit.
- Build a starter emergency fund.
- Invest in skills and qualifications that raise what you can earn.
- Avoid expensive debt, especially loan apps for spending.

**Building (roughly your 30s)**

- Marriage, children, or supporting parents and siblings often arrive.
- Housing becomes a big goal: a better rent, land, or a home of your own.
- Grow the emergency fund to several months of expenses.
- Start long-term saving seriously, even small amounts.

**Peak earning (roughly your 40s and 50s)**

- Income is often at its highest, and so are responsibilities: school fees, university, family.
- Pay down debts so you enter later life owing little.
- Push long-term saving and investing hard. These are the catch-up years.

**Later life (60 and beyond)**

- Live from what you've built: pension, savings, investments, rental income.
- Protect it from scams and risky schemes.
- Plan how your wealth will pass to the people you love.

!> Work on today's stage, and keep one eye on the next.

## Planning across stages

Grace is 28. Her goals across the stages:

- **Now:** a ₦300,000 emergency fund by the end of next year.
- **In her 30s:** land for her own home, and her children's school fees.
- **In her 40s:** her children's university.
- **Later:** a business that pays her an income, plus her pension, so she can choose when to stop working.

She can't fund all of that today. But because she can see the whole road, she knows that the small long-term saving she starts at 28 matters, and she won't be surprised when the big costs arrive.

## The sandwich generation

Many of us support parents and younger siblings while raising our own children. If that's you, plan for it openly: decide how much you can give each month, include it in your goals, and talk about it with your family. Support that's planned is sustainable; support that's unplanned quietly empties your savings.
