Ask someone how much they owe and they'll usually mention one thing: a bank loan, maybe. But debt hides in many places, and the pieces people forget are often the most expensive.

## Everything that counts

A liability is anything you owe. List them all:

- **Bank loans:** personal loans, car loans, mortgages.
- **Loan apps:** quick digital loans, often at very high rates.
- **Buy now, pay later:** phones, appliances or furniture you're paying off in instalments.
- **Salary advances:** money borrowed from your employer against future pay.
- **Credit from traders:** goods taken on credit from your supplier or the shop down the road.
- **Money owed to family and friends:** the ones that don't charge interest, but still need to be paid.
- **Unpaid bills:** school fees, rent arrears, utility bills.

!> You can't control a debt you haven't written down.

## For each one, write four things

1. Who you owe.
2. How much is left to pay.
3. The monthly repayment.
4. The interest or charges, if any.

The last one matters most. Loan apps and instalment plans can cost far more than the price of what you bought, because of fees and interest that are easy to overlook.

## An example

Daniel thought he owed "just the loan app". His full list:

- loan app: ₦85,000 left,
- phone on instalments: ₦120,000 left,
- salary advance: ₦50,000,
- his cousin: ₦40,000,
- school fees arrears: ₦60,000.

Total: **₦355,000**, owed to five different people or companies. It was uncomfortable to see. But now he can plan: in Topic 10 we'll cover how to pay debts down, starting either with the smallest or the most expensive.

## Not all debt is the same

Some borrowing can help you build: a loan for equipment that earns more than it costs, for example. Most consumer debt, borrowed to spend, works against you. We'll look at good and bad debt properly in Topic 10. For now, the job is just to see it all.

## Your turn

Be honest with yourself: how many people or apps do you owe? Write down every one, with the amount left. Add them up. That total is your liabilities, and it goes on your Money Map.
