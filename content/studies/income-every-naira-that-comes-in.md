Ask most people what their income is and they'll tell you their salary. But a salary is usually only part of the picture, and if you plan with only part of the picture, you'll make the wrong decisions.

Topic 2 goes deeper into step 1 of the journey: knowing exactly where you stand. Today we start with what comes in.

## The four kinds of income

1. **Personal income:** what you earn from your work. A salary or wages, overtime, bonuses, allowances, commission.
2. **Business income:** money from something you run. A shop, a trade, freelance work, a side hustle, a small farm.
3. **Investment income:** money your money earns. Interest on savings, rent from a property, dividends from shares, profit when you sell something for more than you paid.
4. **Other income:** money that comes to you without work or investment. Support from family, gifts, grants, scholarships, contributions you receive from an ajo or esusu.

!> Count every naira that comes in, from every source. You can't plan with half the picture.

## An example

Grace works as a receptionist and earns **₦180,000** a month. That's what she says when people ask.

But when she writes down everything for one month:

- Salary: ₦180,000
- Selling small chops at weekends: ₦45,000
- Interest on her savings: ₦3,000
- Her brother's monthly support: ₦20,000

Her real income is **₦248,000**, nearly 40% more than she thought. That changes what she can save, what she can afford and what she can plan for.

## Why every source matters

- **You'll see what you can grow.** Investment income is small for most people at first. Writing it down every month shows whether it's growing, which is the snowball we're trying to build.
- **You'll see what's fragile.** If one source is most of your income, losing it would hurt badly. That's a risk worth knowing about (step 5).
- **You'll stop wasting "extra" money.** Weekend income, gifts and bonuses often disappear because they feel like bonus money. Counted as income, they can go towards your goals.

## Monthly, and take-home

Two rules make your numbers useful:

- **Use monthly figures.** If something comes yearly (a bonus, an annual rent you receive), divide it by 12.
- **Use what actually reaches you.** What lands in your account after deductions, not the figure on your offer letter. That's tomorrow's study.

## Your turn

List every source of income you had last month, with the amount. How many sources do you have? Most people find at least one they'd forgotten.
