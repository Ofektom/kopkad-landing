Topic 3 gave you a destination. Topic 4 gives you the simplest system there is for getting there. It starts with turning one sum around.

## Turn the formula around

Most people do this:

!> Income − spending = whatever is left to save.

Usually nothing is left. Not because they earn too little, but because spending expands to fill whatever is there.

Financially intelligent people do it the other way round:

!> Income − saving and giving = what I can spend.

They decide what goes to the future and to others first, and live on what remains.

## Your income is a pie

Picture every income as a pie. The whole pie is what actually lands in your account: your take-home pay, plus side money, business profit you pay yourself, even a cash gift. Before you eat any of it, you cut it into three slices:

1. **Generosity:** family support, religious giving, helping your community.
2. **Planning for the future:** your emergency fund, your goals, paying off debt faster, long-term saving.
3. **Living:** rent, food, transport, data, bills and some fun.

In the Wealth Builder in the Kopkad app, these are called **Give**, **Savings** and **Living**.

## Grace's pie

Grace takes home ₦200,000 a month. On payday, before she buys anything, she cuts her pie:

- Generosity, 10%: **₦20,000**
- Future, 20%: **₦40,000**
- Living, 70%: **₦140,000**

Her month is planned around ₦140,000, not ₦200,000. The other ₦60,000 already has a job.

## Every income, not just salary

The pie works on any amount, on any day. A ₦15,000 side job on Saturday is a small pie: ₦1,500, ₦3,000 and ₦10,500. Slicing small incomes builds the habit as much as slicing big ones.
