The second slice is the one that changes your life. It's the money you send ahead to your future self, and it only works if it's taken first.

## What goes in the Future slice

- **Emergency fund:** a starter buffer first, then three to six months of your Living costs.
- **Paying off debt faster:** anything you pay above the minimum, especially on expensive debt like loan apps.
- **Your goals:** rent, school fees, business capital, the things you priced in Topic 3.
- **Protection:** health insurance or an HMO plan, if your employer doesn't give you one.
- **Long-term saving:** investments and extra pension contributions, once you have a cushion.

Your minimum loan repayments are a bill, so they sit in the Living slice. The Future slice is where you **speed up** the payoff.

## In what order

Follow the same order as Topic 3:

1. A starter emergency fund.
2. Expensive debt.
3. Your full emergency fund.
4. Goals, by date and importance.
5. Long-term saving, started early, even if small.

You don't have to finish one before starting the next. Most of the slice goes to the current step, and a small amount to a later one.

## Grace's Future slice

Grace's Future slice is **₦40,000** a month:

- ₦15,000 to her starter emergency fund,
- ₦15,000 to her rent goal, due in ten months,
- ₦10,000 extra on her ₦60,000 loan app balance, to clear it sooner.

## Make it automatic

Willpower is unreliable at the end of the month. Move the Future slice on payday, before you spend anything, and if you can, into an account you don't carry around on your phone every day. The less you see it, the less you'll be tempted to touch it.

!> Pay your future self first. Whatever you leave for the end of the month usually isn't there.
