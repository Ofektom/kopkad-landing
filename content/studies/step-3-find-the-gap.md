Money worries feel huge partly because they're vague. "I'll never afford it" isn't a number, so there's nothing to work on.

Step 3 of the journey replaces the fog with one figure: the gap.

## How to work it out

For each goal you wrote down in step 2:

1. **What the goal costs.** The number you wrote.
2. **What you'll have by then if nothing changes.** What you've already saved, plus what you're currently putting aside, times the months left.
3. **The gap** is the first number minus the second.

!> Gap = what the goal costs − what you'll have if nothing changes.

## An example

Esther needs **₦600,000** for her rent, due in **10 months**.

- She already has ₦150,000 saved.
- She's currently saving ₦20,000 a month. Over 10 months that's ₦200,000.
- If nothing changes, she'll have ₦150,000 + ₦200,000 = **₦350,000**.

Her gap is ₦600,000 − ₦350,000 = **₦250,000**.

Spread over the 10 months, that's **₦25,000 a month** more than she's doing now.

That's the moment the fog lifts. "I'll never manage the rent" has turned into "I need to find ₦25,000 a month." That's a problem she can work on.

## What the gap tells you

- **No gap, or a small one?** Your current habits will get you there. Protect them.
- **A gap you can close?** Good. The next step is choosing how.
- **A gap that's far too big?** Don't give up on the goal. Move the date, lower the first target, or break it into stages. A goal you can hit builds the confidence to reach the next one.

## Do the same for your long-term goals

The same maths works for bigger, longer goals, though prices rise over time, so a goal five years away will cost more than today's price. We'll cover how inflation changes the numbers in a later topic. For now, a rough gap is far better than none.
