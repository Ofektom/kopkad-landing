Michael and Mercy both earn **₦300,000** a month. That's about ₦10,000 a day.

Michael spends ₦9,900 of it every day and keeps ₦100. Mercy spends ₦9,000 and keeps ₦1,000.

After 100 days, Michael has **₦10,000** saved. Mercy has **₦100,000**. After a year, Michael has ₦36,500 and Mercy has ₦365,000.

Same salary. Same city. Same prices at the market. Ten times the savings.

## What wealth really is

Your salary is what passes through your hands. Your wealth is what stays: the money you keep, and the things you own that hold or grow their value.

Most of us were taught to measure ourselves by income. "How much do you earn?" is the question everyone asks. But income on its own says very little. Plenty of people on big salaries are one bad month away from borrowing, and plenty of people on modest incomes are quietly building real security.

!> Wealth is what you keep and own, not what you earn.

## So what is financial intelligence?

Financial intelligence is the skill of turning income into wealth. In everyday life it looks like four habits:

1. **Controlling spending.** Deciding what you buy, instead of letting money leave on its own.
2. **Living below your means.** Spending less than comes in, every month, not just in good months.
3. **Choosing needs over wants.** Not "never enjoy anything", but knowing which is which before you pay.
4. **Saving before spending.** Keeping part of every income the day it arrives, before anything else touches it.

Without these habits, a bigger salary usually just funds a bigger lifestyle: a nicer phone, a pricier apartment, more outings. The savings stay small, and freedom from money worries stays out of reach, no matter how much you earn.

## The good news

Mercy isn't luckier than Michael. She made one decision and repeated it every day. That is the whole point of this class: financial intelligence is a skill, and skills can be learned.

Over the next few weeks we'll build it step by step: knowing where you stand, setting goals, closing the gap, protecting what you build, and growing it.
