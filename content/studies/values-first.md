Here's an uncomfortable exercise. Look at where your money went last month, and ask: if a stranger read this list, what would they think I care about?

For many of us, the honest answer doesn't match what we'd say. We say family comes first, but the biggest line is outings with friends. We say security matters most, but nothing went to savings.

That gap isn't about being a bad person. It's what happens when money leaves without a plan.

## What values are

Your values are the things that matter most to you, the things you'd want your life to be about. Common ones include:

- **Family:** providing for and spending time with the people you love.
- **Security:** knowing you're safe if something goes wrong.
- **Freedom:** having choices about your time and work.
- **Faith:** living out your beliefs, including giving.
- **Growth:** learning, building skills, improving yourself.
- **Generosity:** helping others and your community.
- **Adventure:** travel and new experiences.
- **Comfort:** a home and daily life that feel good.

There are no right or wrong values. What matters is knowing yours.

!> Your money should look like what you care about.

## Three steps

**1. Pick your top three.** From the list above, or your own words. Be honest, not impressive.

**2. Look at last month.** Take your spending from Topic 2 and mark each item with the value it served, if any. Rent and food might serve family and comfort. A course serves growth. Many small purchases will serve none of your top three.

**3. Find the gaps.** Where is money going to things you don't really value? Where is a top value getting nothing?

## An example

Mercy's top three values are **family, security and growth**. Her last month:

- family support and her children's things: ₦45,000, which serves family,
- savings: ₦0, so nothing for security,
- a professional course: ₦0, so nothing for growth,
- eating out and drinks with friends: ₦48,000, which isn't in her top three.

She doesn't stop seeing her friends. She cuts eating out to ₦20,000 and moves the ₦28,000 she frees up: ₦18,000 to savings and ₦10,000 towards a course. Now her money looks more like her values.
