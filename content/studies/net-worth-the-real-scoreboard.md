You've listed what you own. You've listed what you owe. Put the two together and you get the most honest number in personal finance.

## The formula

!> Net worth = assets (what you own) − liabilities (what you owe).

That's it. It doesn't care about your job title, your car or your salary. It measures what you'd have left if you sold everything and paid off every debt.

## Working it out

1. **Add up your assets** at today's value: cash, savings, investments, pension, property, business stock and equipment, valuables at what they'd sell for.
2. **Add up your liabilities:** every loan, instalment, advance, credit and unpaid bill.
3. **Subtract** the second from the first.

## Two examples

Mercy:

- assets: ₦420,000 savings + ₦380,000 pension + ₦150,000 phone and laptop = **₦950,000**,
- liabilities: ₦100,000 left on a loan,
- net worth: **₦850,000**.

Daniel:

- assets: ₦60,000 savings + ₦150,000 phone = **₦210,000**,
- liabilities: **₦355,000** across five debts,
- net worth: **−₦145,000**.

## If yours is negative

A negative net worth feels bad, but it's very common, especially early in life or after a hard year. It isn't a verdict on you. It's a starting point, and the most useful thing about it is that you can now watch it change.

If Daniel pays down ₦20,000 of debt and saves ₦10,000 more each month, his net worth rises by ₦30,000 a month. In five months, he's above zero. That's real progress, even though his salary hasn't moved.

## Why it's the real scoreboard

- It shows whether you're actually getting richer, not just earning more.
- It rewards both sides: saving more and owing less.
- It catches problems that a salary hides, like a big income with even bigger debts.
