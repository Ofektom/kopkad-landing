If you sell for a living, money comes in every day. That feels like more money than a salary, but it hides a trap: sales are not income.

## Sales are not income

Joy sells foodstuffs at the market. On a good day she takes in ₦60,000. But most of that pays for the next bag of rice, the next tin of oil, transport, the market levy and her shop rent. If she spends today's sales as if they were hers, she's eating her stock.

Her month looks like this:

- Sales, 26 market days: ₦1,560,000
- Restock, transport, levies, shop rent: ₦1,320,000
- **Profit: ₦240,000**

₦240,000 is the most she can take for herself. Not ₦1.56 million.

## Three steps

1. **Separate the money.** Keep one account for the business and another for you. Sales go into the business account. Restock is paid from there.
2. **Pay yourself a salary.** A fixed amount, on a fixed day, from profit. Weekly works well for traders.
3. **Slice your salary.** Your salary is your pie.

## Joy's plan

From her ₦240,000 monthly profit:

- **₦26,000** goes to a tax pot (see below),
- **₦14,000** stays in the business, to grow stock and cover slow weeks,
- **₦200,000** is her salary: ₦50,000 every Saturday.

Each Saturday she slices her ₦50,000 on the Seed split: **₦5,000** to giving, **₦10,000** to her future, and **₦35,000** to live on for the week.

## Don't forget tax

Traders don't have an employer deducting tax, so the plan has to include it. Under the rules in force since 1 January 2026, the first ₦800,000 of a person's taxable income a year is tax-free, and the next ₦2.2 million is taxed at 15%. Joy's profit of about ₦2.9 million a year would mean roughly ₦310,000 in tax before any reliefs, about ₦26,000 a month. Reliefs such as rent relief or pension contributions can reduce it. Records of your sales and costs help you pay the right amount, not a guess. We'll cover tax in its own series.

!> Sales belong to the business. Your salary belongs to you. Only your salary gets sliced.

## In slow weeks

If a week is slow, the business buffer pays your salary, not your savings. If slow weeks keep coming, lower your salary for a while rather than eating your stock.

## Your turn

Do you mix business and personal money? Work out your average monthly profit over the last three months, and choose a weekly salary you could pay yourself from it.
