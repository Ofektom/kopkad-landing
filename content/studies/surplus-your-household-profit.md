Every business owner knows one number matters more than any other: profit. Did the business make more than it spent?

Your household is no different. It has income coming in and expenses going out. What's left is your profit.

## The formula

!> Income − expenses = surplus (profit) or deficit (loss).

- **A surplus** means you lived on less than you earned. That money is the raw material for every goal you have: savings, an emergency fund, investments, paying down debt.
- **A deficit** means you spent more than came in. The difference came from somewhere: savings you already had, a loan, money from family, unpaid bills.

## Savings rate

To compare months, or compare yourself at different incomes, turn the surplus into a percentage of what came in:

**Savings rate = surplus ÷ income.**

Mercy takes home ₦240,000 and spends ₦192,000. Her surplus is ₦48,000. Her savings rate is ₦48,000 ÷ ₦240,000 = **20%**.

A savings rate is more honest than a salary. Someone earning ₦600,000 who saves nothing is building less than someone earning ₦150,000 who saves ₦15,000 every month.

Many money guides suggest aiming for **20% or more**. If that feels far away, start where you are. Going from 0% to 5% is a big step.

## Why a deficit is dangerous

A deficit month now and then happens to everyone: a funeral, a school fee, a repair. The danger is when deficits become normal. Each one is paid for with borrowing or old savings, so every month you're a little further behind, even if your salary hasn't changed.

If you're in deficit most months, that's not a moral failure. It's information. It tells you the plan needs to change: lower fixed costs, cut some variable spending, raise income, or all three.

## What to do with a surplus

A surplus left in your current account tends to get spent. Give it a job the day you see it:

1. emergency fund first,
2. then your goals with dates,
3. then long-term savings and investments.

That's the pie, which we cover properly in Topic 4: decide your savings first and spend from what remains.
