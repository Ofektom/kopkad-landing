Fixed costs are hard to change. Variable costs are the opposite: you decide them, often several times a day. That makes them the easiest place to take back control, and the easiest place to lose it.

## What counts as variable

Variable expenses change from month to month with your choices:

- food and groceries,
- transport and fuel,
- eating out, drinks, outings,
- clothes and personal care,
- gifts, celebrations and event contributions,
- household items and repairs.

## Why they're slippery

Variable spending rarely feels like a decision. Nobody sits down and says "this month I'll spend ₦60,000 on eating out." It happens a meal at a time. Each purchase is small and feels reasonable, so the total only shows up at the end of the month, when it's too late.

!> Every variable expense is a small decision. Make the decision before the month starts, not at the till.

## Three ways to cut, without suffering

The money course this class is based on calls them **cut costs, cut back, cut out**:

1. **Cut costs:** pay less for the same thing. Buy foodstuffs in bulk with a neighbour, use the cheaper market, switch to a monthly data plan.
2. **Cut back:** do it less often. Eat out twice a month instead of twice a week. Take a ride only when it's raining.
3. **Cut out:** stop the things you don't really value. The subscription you forgot about. The habit you keep out of routine, not pleasure.

You don't have to do all three for everything. Start with one category.

## An example

Joy's variable spending last month:

- Food and groceries: ₦70,000
- Eating out and drinks: ₦42,000
- Transport: ₦35,000
- Clothes and hair: ₦25,000

That's ₦172,000. She decides on three changes:

- buys rice, oil and beans in bulk with her sister: food down to **₦60,000**,
- eats out on Fridays only: down to **₦20,000**,
- leaves clothes and transport as they are this month.

She saves **₦32,000** a month without giving up anything she really cares about.

## Set limits in advance

At the start of each month, give each variable category a limit. Track as you go. When a category runs out, it's out until next month, or you consciously move money from another category. The limit turns dozens of small decisions into one.
