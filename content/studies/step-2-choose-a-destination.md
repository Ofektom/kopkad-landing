"I want to save more." "I want to be comfortable." "I want to own my own place someday."

These are wishes. There's nothing wrong with them, but you can't plan towards a wish. Step 2 of the journey is turning wishes into destinations.

## What makes a real goal

A money goal you can actually reach has three parts:

1. **A number.** How much does it cost, in naira?
2. **A date.** By when do you need it?
3. **A reason.** Why does it matter to you?

"Save more" becomes **"₦600,000 for next year's rent by 1 January."** Now you can work out what you need to put aside each month, and you'll know whether you're on track.

!> A goal without a number is a wish. A goal with a number and a date is a plan waiting to happen.

## Goals for every stage of life

Your destination isn't just one big goal. Most people have several, at different distances. Here are examples to get you thinking (the amounts are illustrations; use your own):

- **Short term (within a year):** a ₦150,000 emergency cushion, rent, school fees for next term, clearing a small debt.
- **Medium term (one to five years):** a professional certification, a deposit on land, stock for a business, a wedding.
- **Long term (five years and beyond):** your own home, your children's university, being able to stop working one day without worrying about money.

Think across the areas of your life too: your education and career, your family and relationships, where you live, how you want to spend your free time, and the day you no longer have to worry about money.

## Goals must match your values

A goal you don't really care about won't survive the first tempting weekend. Ask yourself why each goal matters. If the honest answer is "because everyone else is doing it", it may belong to someone else.

Your goals also need to fit your real income, which is why step 1 comes first. A goal that would take 90% of your income isn't a goal; it's a source of guilt. Start with targets you can hit, then raise them.

## Write them down

Pick up to three goals. For each one, write:

- what it is,
- how much it costs,
- the date you need it by,
- why it matters to you.

Keep the list somewhere you'll see it. On Monday, step 3 uses these numbers to find your gap.
