Michael's offer letter says **₦300,000 a month**. So why does his bank alert only show about ₦242,000?

Because there are two salaries: the one you're offered, and the one you actually receive.

## Gross and net

- **Gross income** is your full salary before anything is taken out.
- **Net income**, or take-home pay, is what's left after deductions. It's what actually reaches your account.

!> Plan with your net income. You can't spend, save or give money that never reaches you.

## The usual deductions

If you're employed in Nigeria, the common deductions are:

- **Pension:** at least 8% of your pensionable pay goes into your Retirement Savings Account, and your employer adds at least 10% of their own. This money is still yours, saved for later.
- **National Housing Fund (NHF):** 2.5% of your basic salary, where your employer deducts it.
- **PAYE tax:** income tax, taken by your employer and paid to the government for you.
- **Others:** health insurance (HMO), cooperative contributions, loan repayments taken straight from your salary.

## Today's tax rules (since 1 January 2026)

Nigeria's income tax changed on **1 January 2026**, when the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 took effect. If you learned the old rules, a lot has moved. Under the current law:

- the first **₦800,000** of taxable income a year is tax-free,
- income above that is taxed in bands: 15% on the next ₦2.2 million, then 18%, 21% and 23%, up to **25% only on taxable income above ₦50 million**,
- pension and NHF contributions still reduce the income you're taxed on,
- you can claim **rent relief** of 20% of the rent you pay, up to ₦500,000 a year, if you can show receipts.

**What's gone:** the old *consolidated relief allowance* (CRA), the automatic ₦200,000-plus-20% deduction many payslips used to show, no longer exists. Rent relief replaced it, so if you rent and haven't given your employer your receipts, you may be paying more tax than you need to.

**Beware of rumours:** claims that "everyone will pay 25% tax" are false. The 25% band applies only to the part of a very high income above ₦50 million a year. If a tax claim sounds alarming, check it against an official source before you act on it.

## Michael's payslip, roughly

Michael earns ₦300,000 a month, with a basic salary of ₦150,000, and pays ₦600,000 a year in rent:

- Pension, 8%: **₦24,000**
- NHF, 2.5% of basic: **₦3,750**
- PAYE, after his pension, NHF and rent relief: about **₦29,700**

His take-home is about **₦242,500**. That's the number he should plan with, not ₦300,000.

Your own figures depend on how your pay is structured, so check your payslip. If your rent relief isn't on it, ask your HR or payroll team how to submit your rent receipts.

## If you're self-employed

You don't have a payslip, but the same idea applies. Your "gross" is what your business takes in; your "net" is what's left after business costs and tax. Plan your personal life with the net.

## Your turn

Do you know your take-home pay to the naira? Find your last payslip or salary alert and write down both numbers: gross and net. From now on, plan with the net.
